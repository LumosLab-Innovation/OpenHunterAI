# Americas and Africa reference expansion

Reviewed on **2026-10-08**. This batch contains **21 jurisdictions, 21 frameworks, 14 executable requirement packs and 201 selected clauses**. It adds a financial-sector US framework alongside the existing FIPS impact classification and CSF governance tiers.

## Interpretation and evidence boundary

- `baseline` means a declared assessment target for selected obligations. It is not an official national grade.
- Country and sector applicability, controller/processor roles, CII designation, exceptions and reporting triggers need explicit evidence and manual review. A country code does not settle these questions.
- Statutory `shall` requirements and supervisory `should` recommendations remain distinct. Canada and Kenya guidance is encoded as recommendations. Ecuador's optional listed security mechanisms remain recommendations.
- All packs have partial coverage. Empty or expired evidence remains a gap; declared facts and opaque references do not establish implementation, a successful pentest, certification or compliance.
- Legal/regulatory entries use `requires_review`: this review did not exhaust amendments, implementing rules, sector decisions or binding regulator instructions. Uruguay's `reviewed_reference` records its checked public framework; it does not establish absence of other laws.
- No active scan, account access, credential storage, external LLM completion or scan-permission change was performed for this expansion.

## Verification completed

The final batch was merged with the existing catalog **in memory**, preserving other agents' files. `validateCatalog` and `validateRequirementPack` accept all14 packs. Template creation and empty-evidence assessment were exercised for every supported target:18 target selections including Uruguay0–4. Every result retained `conclusion: not_determined` and `formalLevelDetermination: false`.

Seven catalog entries intentionally have `mappingCoverage: overview_only` and no pack: Argentina, Colombia, Costa Rica, Panama, Nigeria, Tunisia and Egypt. This is an explicit research/implementation gap, not evidence that these jurisdictions lack cybersecurity obligations. Each shipped pack is partial and uses its own national or regulator source; generic NIST controls were not duplicated to manufacture jurisdiction coverage.
